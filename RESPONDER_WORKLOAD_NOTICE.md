# Supporting sustainable on-call

Responding to incidents takes focus, energy, and time to recover. On-Call Health helps teams recognize demanding periods early, share responsibilities, and plan coverage that gives responders room to recharge.

The goal is to prevent overload and support sustainable on-call work. Workload information and responder feedback give teams a shared starting point for practical conversations about coverage, priorities, and support.

## Bringing workload into view

Depending on the integrations your team enables, On-Call Health uses:

- **Incident and on-call activity:** incident volume and severity, response times, after-hours pages, and on-call schedules from Rootly or PagerDuty.
- **Workload activity:** commits, pull requests, reviews, and activity timing from GitHub, and assigned work from Jira or Linear.
- **Your check-ins:** the responses you submit through Slack or On-Call Health, including how you are feeling and any optional stress factors, personal context, or comments you choose to share.

These activity signals inform individual On-Call Health (OCH) scores and trends. Check-ins add your perspective so the team can understand how that workload feels to you. Information is associated with your name, email, or connected account identifiers.

## Turning information into support

Teams can use this information to discuss sharing incident coverage, adjusting upcoming shifts, reducing competing work, or allowing recovery time after a demanding week. Your feedback provides context that activity data alone cannot capture.

An OCH score is a workload planning signal, not a medical assessment or a measure of an individual's performance. Interpret it alongside the responder's experience and circumstances. Decisions about support and scheduling belong in a conversation with the people involved.

## Who can see the information

People with access to your team's analyses can see scores, workload trends, and check-in responses associated with individual responders' names or emails. Depending on your organization's access arrangements, this can include team leads, managers, and other organization members with analysis access.

Where the Rootly MCP health-risk integration is enabled, authorized callers can retrieve existing individual scores and compare them with on-call schedules through tools such as Claude or Codex.

This lets the person planning coverage consider existing workload before assigning more on-call time. The integration shares names, scores, scheduled workload, and coverage suggestions with the connected assistant. It reads existing analyses and schedules without automatically changing shifts.

## Keeping your perspective part of the conversation

If a trend does not reflect your experience, raise that with your team lead and discuss what would help. Optional check-in questions let you choose how much additional context to share; the responses you submit are visible to people with access to your team's analyses.

Your team's On-Call Health administrator can explain which integrations are enabled, who can access results, and how to change check-in settings. Workload analysis also uses connected activity, including when you do not submit a check-in.

## For teams introducing On-Call Health

Managers are advised to hold a meeting with their team when introducing On-Call Health. Explain the goal of preventing overload, which data sources will be used, who can see individual results, and how the information will support coverage and workload planning. Include any enabled MCP access in that explanation, and invite responders to ask questions and discuss the support they need.

Provide the team with a link to this guide during the meeting and in a follow-up message, before using individual results or enabling MCP access. Share the link directly with anyone who cannot attend, including responders who do not receive check-ins, so everyone whose workload is analyzed has the same information.
